# Romania 1936 — Opening Strategy

## Situation Overview

Romania starts in 1936 as a mid-sized but militarily weak power sitting in one of the most dangerous neighborhoods in the game:

- **Hungary** has revisionist claims on Transylvania (part of Romania).
- **Bulgaria** has claims on Southern Dobruja.
- **The USSR** has claims on Bessarabia.
- Romania is nominally part of the **Little Entente** (with Czechoslovakia and Yugoslavia), which will collapse once Czechoslovakia gets dismantled by Germany.

Romania's biggest strategic asset is its **oil fields around Ploiești** — this makes Romania very attractive to Germany, and historically Germany will often guarantee Romania's independence to secure that oil. You start with an outdated army, weak industry, and some internal political instability (tension between King Carol II's regime, the fascist Iron Guard, and other factions).

## Step 1: Decide Your Political Path Early

This is the single most important decision, since it shapes your entire game:

1. **Historical/Fascist path (Iron Guard → Ion Antonescu, join the Axis)** — Easiest and most guided path. You end up aligned with Germany, eventually get war goals against the USSR to reclaim Bessarabia, and can participate in Barbarossa. Good if you want a straightforward, story-driven playthrough with strong industrial/military backing from Germany.
2. **Stay under King Carol II / democratic-leaning path** — More flexible, lets you try to stay neutral longer or eventually align with the Allies, but you'll face more internal instability and less initial outside support. Harder mode.
3. **Communist path** — Least historical and hardest to pull off from Romania's starting position; only worth it if you specifically want the challenge.

For a first game, the **fascist/Axis-aligned path** is the recommended choice: it's the most forgiving, gives you clear focus tree guidance toward stabilizing the government, and sets you up to eventually get German equipment/support and justified war goals on your neighbors (especially the USSR for Bessarabia, and potentially Hungary depending on how the Vienna Award-type events play out).

## Step 2: First Focuses (Priority Order)

Regardless of exact focus names (these can shift slightly between patches/DLC), aim for this general sequence:

1. **Political stabilization focuses first.** Romania starts with government instability — your first couple of focuses should target political power gain and stability so you aren't wasting time on internal strife. This also unlocks the ability to pick your ideological direction (Iron Guard vs. King Carol's camp).
2. **Economic/industry focuses.** Get civilian factory and infrastructure-boosting focuses going almost immediately — Romania's industrial base is weak, and every extra civilian factory early compounds over the whole game.
3. **Oil-focused focuses (Ploiești).** Prioritize any focus that boosts oil production or refining. This is Romania's unique strength — more oil means more fuel for your (eventually mechanized/armored) forces and makes you far more valuable to Germany as an ally, which usually gets you better trade deals and equipment access.
4. **Army reform / modernization focuses.** Your starting divisions use obsolete equipment. Focuses that improve training, unlock better equipment lines, or grant experience will matter a lot since you're bordered by hostile Hungary and the USSR.
5. **Diplomatic/alignment focuses toward the Axis.** Once your internal politics are stable, work toward the focuses that formally align you with Germany/the Axis — this typically brings a guarantee of independence (protecting you from Hungary or the USSR jumping you early) plus access to better equipment and possibly license production.

## Step 3: Diplomatic Priorities

- **Get a guarantee/protection from Germany as early as possible.** Until then, you are vulnerable — Hungary in particular can be aggressive about Transylvania.
- **Don't provoke the USSR early.** You are far too weak in 1936-38 to fight the Soviets alone; wait until you have German backing (or at minimum a much stronger army) before any Bessarabia-related war goal.
- **Watch Yugoslavia and the Little Entente.** These alliances will likely become irrelevant once Czechoslovakia falls, so don't over-invest political capital keeping them alive.

## Step 4: Military & Economic Priorities

- **Build civilian factories before military factories early on** — your industrial base needs to grow before it's worth pumping out equipment.
- **Use your starting infantry equipment stock efficiently**; don't rush into expensive division templates. Basic infantry with support companies is fine until your industry catches up.
- **Fortify your borders**, especially with Hungary, using construction focuses/fortification building once available — a few levels of forts along the Transylvania border buys you a lot of safety.
- **Research priority:** infantry equipment upgrades and industry techs (construction, factory output) first; hold off on expensive tanks/aircraft lines until your factory count can support them.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Don't declare war on anyone before you're aligned with a major power.** Romania alone cannot handle Hungary + USSR pressure simultaneously.
- **Don't neglect political stability focuses** — skipping them to rush military focuses often leaves you with civil unrest or a weak government that can't declare war/join factions effectively.
- **Don't ignore oil.** It's Romania's trump card diplomatically and economically — leverage it.
- **Don't over-commit to the Little Entente** diplomatically; it's a relic that mostly evaporates once Czechoslovakia is partitioned.

## Summary Priority List (first ~5-8 focuses)

1. Stabilize government / gain political power
2. Choose ideological direction (recommend: lean fascist/Iron Guard for a guided first playthrough)
3. Civilian factory / infrastructure focus
4. Ploiești oil output focus
5. Army modernization/training focus
6. Second industry or oil-related focus
7. Move toward German guarantee/Axis alignment
8. Fortification/border security focus (Hungary border)

This gets Romania stable, industrially stronger, oil-optimized, and protected by a major power before the late-1930s crises (Anschluss, Czechoslovakia, Poland) start reshaping the map around you.
