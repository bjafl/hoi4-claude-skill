# Why Germany runs out of supply pushing into Russia (and how to fix it)

Running out of supply during Barbarossa is one of the most common HOI4 problems, and it's almost always a combination of the same handful of causes. Here's the breakdown.

## Why it happens

1. **Soviet infrastructure is terrible.** Most USSR states, especially away from the core western border, have low infrastructure levels (often 1-4). Supply throughput through a state scales with infrastructure, so as soon as your spearheads move past the initial border states, the supply network chokes even though your rear areas (Germany, Poland) are fine.

2. **You're outrunning your own supply lines.** Fast armor/motorized spearheads can advance hundreds of kilometers in days, but supply has to physically route from a supply hub through the state network to your divisions. If you push faster than you extend/build hubs and railways, the front outpaces supply.

3. **Too many divisions stacked on too few supply routes.** The USSR interior has a sparse rail/road network. If you funnel a huge army through one or two corridors, you overload the limited throughput of that path, even if aggregate infrastructure elsewhere looks okay.

4. **Motorized/mechanized-heavy templates consume a lot more supply per division than infantry**, and armor also burns fuel. If your spearhead force is very tank/mot heavy without logistics support, consumption spikes right when your supply lines are already stressed.

5. **No supply hubs in captured territory.** Supply hubs are the nodes that actually distribute supply forward. If you haven't built new ones (or captured/repaired existing Soviet ones) near the front, supply has to travel too far from your last usable hub.

6. **Partisan/resistance activity** in newly occupied Soviet territory disrupts the supply network further if you haven't garrisoned it or built up compliance.

7. **Weather.** Rasputitsa (mud season, spring/autumn) and winter both severely cut supply throughput across the Eastern Front. Even a "working" supply setup can collapse for a few weeks during mud season.

8. **Fuel shortage compounding the problem.** This isn't the same system as supply, but it hits at the same time: German armor stalling from lack of fuel looks and feels like a supply problem. Low oil stockpiles plus long distances from synthetic refineries make this worse the deeper you push.

## How to fix it

**Before you invade:**
- Stockpile fuel (build synthetic refineries, secure oil from Romania/Ploesti, and bank a fuel reserve) so your panzer divisions don't stall a few weeks in.
- Make sure your jump-off infrastructure (Poland, the border states) is upgraded — infrastructure investment here pays off immediately once you cross into the USSR.
- Give your spearhead divisions a **Logistics Company** support company. It directly reduces supply consumption per division, which is the single best "set and forget" fix for armor/motorized formations.
- Pick a general with the **Logistics Wizard** trait for your main invasion army if you can get one; it further cuts supply strain for that army.
- If you're using Mobile Warfare doctrine, make sure you've taken the logistics-related bonuses in that tree.

**During the invasion:**
- **Build railways continuously as you advance.** Use engineers/construction to extend rail from your existing network into newly captured states. Don't wait until supply is already critical — start extending infra as soon as a state is secured.
- **Build (or repair) supply hubs near the front**, not just far in the rear. A hub close to the line drastically shortens the "last mile" that has to travel over poor local infrastructure.
- **Widen your logistics corridor.** Instead of funneling your whole army through one rail line/road, spread the offensive across multiple axes so you're not saturating a single throughput-limited path.
- **Don't overextend the spearhead.** Periodically pause fast units to let rail/hub construction catch up, rather than continuously sprinting toward Moscow. A controlled "leapfrog" advance (push, consolidate/build, push again) keeps supply positive instead of deeply negative.
- **Garrison captured territory** and manage compliance/resistance so partisans aren't cutting your rail lines and hub throughput behind the front.
- **Trim division supply need where you can** — don't make every division heavy armor/motorized; a core of infantry (which needs much less supply) holding ground while a smaller mechanized/armor fist does the breakthroughs eases total consumption.
- **Time major pushes around mud season** if possible — expect a supply (and speed) penalty during rasputitsa and don't panic-diagnose it as a permanent problem; it's largely weather-driven and will ease up.

## Quick checklist
- [ ] Fuel stockpile built up before Barbarossa starts
- [ ] Logistics Company support company on spearhead divisions
- [ ] Railway construction actively extending behind the advance
- [ ] New/repaired supply hubs near the current front line
- [ ] Advance split across multiple corridors, not one funnel
- [ ] Rear areas garrisoned to control resistance
- [ ] Not pure motorized/armor — mixed with lower-supply infantry for holding

If you do most of these, supply in Russia stays manageable even on a fast timeline; skipping the railway/hub construction step is the single most common reason players "run out" mid-offensive.
