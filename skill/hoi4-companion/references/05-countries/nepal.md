Nepal is a landlocked minor country in the Himalayas, bordered by the British Raj to the south, east, and west, and Tibet to the north. With minimal industrial capacity and only one state, Nepal has very limited expansion opportunities and must pursue a careful diplomatic strategy to survive major-power competition in South Asia.

## Government and political situation

Nepal starts as a Non-Aligned authoritarian monarchy under **Juddha Rana** of the Royal House of Gorkha, with no elections. The country maintains a neutral foreign policy historically.

Four political parties exist with alternative ideologies:
- **Democratic:** Bhakti Thapa — would rename the country to Federal Republic of Nepal
- **Communist:** Bir Shamsher Singh Thapa — would rename to Federal Democratic Republic of Nepal
- **Fascist:** Amar Thapa — would rename to Gorkha Empire
- **Non-Aligned (ruling):** Juddha Rana — maintains Nepal as the Kingdom

The wiki does not document specific political conversion paths available through the focus tree or internal decisions.

## Unique mechanics

Nepal lacks a unique national focus tree and uses the generic focus tree instead, which offers five main branches: Army Effort, Aviation Effort, Naval Effort, Industrial Effort, and Political Effort. No special decisions or country-specific mechanics are documented on the wiki.

## Geography and military

Nepal owns a single mountainous state with Kathmandu as its capital. The terrain is entirely mountain (7 provinces). The country starts with **2 mountain divisions** (Paidala Dibhijana) and 16,000 manpower. It has no navy or air force.

Adjacent to mountainous terrain, Nepal's military is naturally suited to mountain warfare, reflected in the Royal Nepalese Army spirit's special forces bonuses.

Nepal begins with minimal heavy industry: only 1 military factory and 2 civilian factories. Three research slots can be unlocked through the focus tree. The country exports 1 Chromium to the market.

## Diplomacy and borders

Nepal borders:
- **British Raj** (south, east, west) — the dominant regional power during the interwar period
- **Tibet** (north)
- **Bhutan** (east, separated by the Indian state of West Bengal)

The country pursues a neutral foreign policy and is not part of any faction at game start.
