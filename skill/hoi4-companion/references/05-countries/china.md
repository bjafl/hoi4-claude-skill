China is a regional power in East Asia at the centre of internal strife and foreign pressure. Despite massive population and territorial extent, China is fractured across warlord cliques and facing imminent Japanese invasion, requiring rapid political stabilization and military reform to survive.

## Government and political situation

Chiang Kai-Shek leads the Kuomintang as a despotic regime, controlling only the Nanjing government. The Republic of China began nominal unification in 1928 after the Northern Expedition but remains fractured by:

- **Warlord cliques:** Yunnan, Guangxi, Guangdong, Shanxi, Shandong, Hebei-Chahar, Sichuan, Northeastern Army, and Muslim Ma cliques (Ningxia, Gansu, Qinghai, Khotan, Sinkiang) — each partially autonomous and capable of being animated as separate war targets.
- **Communist China:** Controls territory in the north; ongoing civil war until Japanese invasion forces a Second United Front.
- **Political instability:** Low starting stability (20%) and multiple competing ideological factions accessible via national focus tree.

China can pursue paths toward **Democracy** (via China Democratic League under various presidents), **Fascism** (Blue Shirt Society under Dai Li), or remain **Non-Aligned** under Chiang Kai-Shek. The national focus tree allows shifting ideologies and replacing the ruler, with Wang Jingwei available as an alternative Non-Aligned leader.

Key advisors available:
- **Political:** Lin Sen (Popular Figurehead: +15% Stability), H.H. Kung (Captain of Industry: +10% civilian/infrastructure/railway/refinery construction), Chiang Ching-Kuo (Silent Workhorse: +15% Political Power gain).
- **Military:** Zhu De (Army Offense Expert), Li Zongren (Army Defense Genius), Alexander von Falkenhausen (German advisor, Army Offense Expert).

## Unique mechanics

**Chinese Power Struggle:** Communists and warlords compete with Nationalist China for control of states via political power expenditure. Communist uprisings can spontaneously transfer states to Communist China if ideology support rises; warlords can be pressured into subject status and later annexed.

**Warlord Integration:** Warlord subjects can be integrated into Nationalist China, unlocking their commanders and resources. Integration is time-consuming and grants political power penalties during the process.

**Chinese United Front:** Nationalist China can form a faction with warlords and communists via national focus or events, combining their military forces against Japan and other enemies. The United Front has exclusive access to certain diplomatic and military focuses.

**Inflation system:** China starts with "Low Inflation" and gains additional inflation modifiers from industrial/welfare/resource-extraction focuses. Decisions (e.g. "Forced Loans") can reduce inflation at political power and stability cost. Reaching "Economic Collapse" locks certain focuses until inflation is managed.

**Multiple focus tree paths:** China's national focus tree branches into **Democratic** (ending in multi-party democracy with elected presidents), **Wang Jingwei** (despotic collaboration path), **Chiang Kai-Shek historical** (remains non-aligned nationalist), and **Blue Shirt Fascist** (Dai Li's authoritarian faction). Several branches are mutually exclusive; players must choose early which ideology and leadership to pursue.

**Diplomatic missions:** Focuses unlock tiers of cooperation with foreign powers (Germany, Soviet Union, France, United States, etc.), granting design companies, volunteer groups, military factories, generals, and technology bonuses contingent on maintaining relations above 75.

**Hidden focus:** "Retreat to Taiwan" unlocks if China is 70% towards capitulation, at war with Communist China, controls Taiwan fully, and has lost control of Nanjing and Chongqing — grants 5 building slots and one factory of each type to Taiwan.

See [06-focus-trees/china.md](../06-focus-trees/china.md) for the full focus tree structure.

## Neighbours and diplomatic position

**Bordering countries:**
- North: Warlord states (Shanxi, Hebei-Chahar, Shandong Clique) and Communist China; Manchukuo (puppet of Japan).
- West: Sichuan Clique, Northeastern Army, Xibei San Ma (Muslim cliques), Sinkiang; Soviet Union (distant, via Sinkiang).
- South: Yunnan Clique, Guangxi Clique, Guangdong Clique; French Indochina; British holdings (Burma, Malaya).
- East: Korea (Japanese puppet), Japan.

**Alliances and guarantees:**
- Starts **unaligned** with no guarantees or faction membership.
- Can form the **Chinese United Front** via events (Warlord Meeting, Communist negotiation) or national focuses, absorbing warlord and communist military forces into a unified faction.
- **Nine Power Treaty** restricts China to Free Trade law and prevents war goal justification against signatories without focus completion.
- **Japan** is the primary military threat; the Marco Polo Bridge Incident typically fires in mid-1937, demanding territorial concessions or triggering full-scale war.
- **Soviet Union** can offer aid via focuses and volunteers if relations are maintained.
- **Germany** can supply military advisors, design companies, and limited volunteers.
- **France** provides military mission bonuses and is available as a patron until its capitulation (~1940–41).
- **United States** can supply volunteers and aid if China allies with the Allies, but requires careful diplomatic work.
