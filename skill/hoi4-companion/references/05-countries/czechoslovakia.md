## Summary

Czechoslovakia is a small, landlocked democratic country in Central Europe with a population of 15.04 million but limited military-industrial capacity. Surrounded by powerful potential enemies (Germany, Austria, Hungary, Romania) and only weakly protected by France and the United Kingdom, Czechoslovakia occupies a precarious position on the eve of WWII. Its government is fractious and internally divided, with competing Czech, Slovak, and German interests pulling in different directions, but it possesses a decent industrial base (particularly the renowned Škoda Works) and access to key resources like steel and coal.

## Government and political situation

Czechoslovakia starts as a democratic conservatist state, governed by a coalition (Široká koalice vedená RSZML). The starting leader Milan Hodža holds the title "Disoriented Foreign Affairs Minister" and represents the agrarian wing of Czech politics.

Alternative leaders accessible through the focus tree include:

- **Edvard Beneš** (Conservatism) — A diplomatic workhorse and de facto leader who provides political power gains and improved diplomatic maintenance costs, available via the [King of the Castle] focus or as the 1936 leader when Peace for Our Time is disabled.
- **Jan Syrový** (Conservatism) — "Reluctant Prime Minister" with mobilization bonuses, accessible via the Crisis of Confidence event.
- **Klement Gottwald** (Stalinism) — Available through focus tree paths [The Köhler Faction] or [The Gottwald Faction].
- **Radola Gajda** (Fascism) — Via the [Power to the Military] focus.
- **Andrej Hlinka** (Non-Aligned/Oligarchic) — Slovak autonomist leader; starts as the 1936 Non-Aligned leader when Peace for Our Time is disabled.
- **Emil Hácha** (Nazism/Non-Aligned) — 3rd President; available through focus tree paths, becomes puppeted in historical path.

The country faces chronic political instability with competing factions: the Czech democratic mainstream, Slovak autonomists (particularly the HSĽS-SNS), communists, and fascists. All four ideologies are present in parliament with significant support.

## Unique mechanics

Czechoslovakia can form the **Austria-Hungary** formable nation if the player conquers Austria, Hungary, and required states from Yugoslavia and Romania. This is available through the Austro-Hungarian joint focus tree branch (available with Götterdämmerung DLC).

The focus tree includes extensive political and diplomatic branches:
- **Evaluate the Political Climate** — Choose path toward communism, fascism, or alignment with major powers.
- **Strategic Decisions** — Manage diplomatic relations and territorial disputes.
- **Fortification Studies** — Defensive focuses to strengthen border fortifications (note: some foci set fort level rather than adding forts, so building additional forts is not recommended).
- **A Great Industrial Legacy** — Industrial and factory output bonuses.
- **Military Technical Institute & Aeronautical Institute** — Research bonuses for ground and air forces.

With Peace for Our Time DLC, the tree has expanded significantly with additional branches for coalition building, military reforms, and strategic positioning.

See [06-focus-trees/czechoslovakia.md](../06-focus-trees/czechoslovakia.md) for the full focus tree.

## Neighbours and diplomatic position

**Bordering countries:**
- Germany (west and north)
- Poland (northeast)
- Austria (south)
- Hungary (south)
- Romania (south)

**Diplomacy:**
- Czechoslovakia is a neutral country and begins unaligned.
- Guarantees the independence of Romania and Yugoslavia.
- Receives guarantees from France and Romania.
- Can join the Little Entente via French event or form its own Czech Entente faction through the focus tree.
- **The Little Entente:** If France pursues the Strengthen the Little Entente focus, Czechoslovakia automatically joins. France may then invite Yugoslavia and Romania (90% accept rate).
- **Czech Entente:** Czechoslovakia can form its own rival faction through the An Entente of Our Own focus, with similar invitation mechanics for Yugoslavia and Romania.
- Historically, Czechoslovakia is in a precarious position: France and the UK prioritize maintaining peace with Germany over guaranteeing Czechoslovak sovereignty, offering only token protection.
