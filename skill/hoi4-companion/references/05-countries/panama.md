Panama is a small, split Central American nation strategically positioned between Costa Rica and Colombia, bisected by the United States–controlled Panama Canal. With only 0.47M population, one state, and minimal industrial capacity, Panama begins as a democratic nation guaranteed independence by the United States under the Monroe Doctrine. Its primary path to relevance is forming Gran Colombia by annexing Colombia, Venezuela, Ecuador, and northern Peru.

## Government and political situation

Harmodio Arias Madrid leads the ruling Partido Liberal Doctrinario (Democratic). The Partido del Pueblo de Panamá (Communist, Bartolomé Sinibaldi) holds 0% popularity; Fascist (Tiburcio Andino) and Non-Aligned (Aniceto Andino) factions are dormant. Elections are held every 4 years.

## National spirits

**Monroe Doctrine:** Panama's independence is guaranteed by the United States. This doctrine protects Panama from unilateral aggression but limits diplomatic independence.

## Unique mechanics

Panama can form **Gran Colombia** by controlling Colombia, Venezuela, Ecuador, and northern Peru (Peru's state 537). Upon formation, Panama gains cores on those four territories plus Panama Canal (state 685) and British Guyana (state 687).

No unique national focus tree—Panama uses the Generic focus tree with five main branches: Army Effort, Aviation Effort, Naval Effort, Industrial Effort, and Political Effort.

## Neighbours and diplomatic position

**Borders:** Costa Rica (west), Colombia (east); the Panama Canal zone is controlled by the United States.

**Faction:** Unaligned (not in any faction in 1936).

**Guarantees:** Independence guaranteed by United States via Monroe Doctrine.

Panama lacks military strength to pursue expansion alone. The formation of Gran Colombia path requires either diplomatic cooperation with Central American neighbors or military conquest across multiple nations—a challenging undertaking with 0 starting divisions and 1 military factory.
