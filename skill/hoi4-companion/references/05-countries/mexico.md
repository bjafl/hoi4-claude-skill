Mexico is a middle-sized North American nation with 16.42M population. It begins 1936 in political instability but with significant diplomatic protection from the United States. The country faces imminent risk of internal civil war and must navigate competing factions before expanding externally. Mexico possesses a unique focus tree offering multiple ideological paths, including the historic opportunity to host Leon Trotsky and pursue communist revolution across the Americas.

## Government and political situation

In 1936, Mexico is governed by the **Partido Nacional Revolucionario** (Non-Aligned), led by **Lázaro Cárdenas**. Three competing parties claim significant support: the Democratic **Partido Liberal Constitucionalista** (18%), the **Unión Nacional Sinarquista** (Fascist, 20%), and the **Partido Comunista Mexicano** (9%). The ruling party holds 53% of popularity. Elections are scheduled for June 1940.

**Notable alternative leaders:**
- Francisco Múgica (Non-Aligned, Revolutionary General) — available in 1940 election; offers army bonuses (Resistance Growth +25%, Conscription/Economy Laws Cost -25%)
- Manuel Ávila Camacho (Oligarchic/Conservative) — Stability +5%, diplomacy cost reduction
- Saturnino Cedillo (Despotism) — Division Recovery +5%, Political Power +10%
- Plutarco E. Calles (can be Despot or Fascist via focus "Jefe Máximo") — Political cost reduction
- Leon Trotsky (Leninism, Communist path only) — Subversive cost -50%, Ideology drift defense +30%, requires invitation via communist focuses

**Ideological paths:** Mexico can pursue Democracy (Partido Liberal Constitucionalista), Communism (Partido Comunista Mexicano, possibly with Trotsky), Fascism (Unión Nacional Sinarquista), or remain Non-Aligned. Focus tree offers distinct branches for each path, including regime changes and leader replacements.

## National spirits

Mexico begins with five national spirits reflecting its recent history:

**Callistas** — Political Power gain -20%, Construction Speed -10.00%. Reflects the lingering influence of Plutarco Calles' faction despite his removal from power in 1934.

**Weak Church** — Oil Concessions +10.00%. The Church's power is diminished after the Cristero War (1926–1929).

**Politicized Army** — Military Leaders Cost +100.0%, Planning Speed -20.0%. The military establishment remains fractious and hard to control.

**Caudillo Tensions Moderate** — Stability -5.00%. General Saturnino Cedillo and other regional strongmen pose an immediate civil-war threat. The tension meter increases over time; managing or suppressing it is critical.

**Monroe Doctrine** — Mexico is guaranteed by the United States and cannot be targeted for conquest (guarantee remains until the US is at war with Mexico or a Faction member).

## Unique mechanics

**Civil War Risk:** Mexico faces up to three internal civil wars:
1. **Caudillo Civil War** — Most likely. A timer increases "Caudillo Tensions" unless specific focuses are taken (Support General Cedillo, Arrest General Cedillo). Letting the timer expire or failing to manage it triggers a revolt.
2. **Cristero Civil War** — Triggered by completing certain focuses and reaching "Atheist State" (Church power reduced) with stability below 60%. Democratic governments are exempt.
3. **Communist Civil War** — Triggered by reaching "Catholic State" (Church power high) with stability below 60%, if the country is not Communist. Communist governments are exempt.

**Church Power System:** Several focuses and decisions add or remove Church influence, affecting which civil wars can occur. The decision "Nationalize Church Lands" (available at start) allows cost-free adjustment of Church power without direct focus requirements.

**National Focus Tree:** Mexico has a unique five-branch tree:
- **National Bank Branch** — Infrastructure, removal of Caudillo civil-war risk, or switch to Communism. Unlocks the Banco de México industrial company.
- **Military Budget Review Sub-branch** — Military technology discounts.
- **Oil Field Expansion Sub-branch** — Foreign relations and faction joining. Unlocks Pemex industrial company.
- **Plan of Agua Prieta Branch** — Ideology switches (Democracy, Fascism, Communism). Includes Church power controls.
- **Control the Army & March Southwards Sub-branches** — Army purges and war goals against Central/South American countries and the southwestern United States (Mexican Cession).

**Expansionist War Goals:** Through "March Southwards" focuses, Mexico can gain cores on Central America, the Caribbean, and the American Southwest (Texas, New Mexico, Arizona, California), and can justify claims on British Honduras.

**Historical Event: Trotsky Invitation** — If pursuing the Communist path, an event offers the chance to invite Leon Trotsky to Mexico. Accepting makes him available as a Chief of Army with strong communist bonuses and division organization. Trotsky can later become country leader.

**Industrial/Design Companies:**
- Banco de México (Refining + Coal bonus, requires "National Bank" focus)
- Pemex (Refining + Infrastructure bonus, requires "Nationalize Oil Fields" focus)
- Escuela de Ingeniería (Electronics, requires "Engineering School" focus)
- Talleres Generales de Arma (Tank design, requires "Tank Workshops" focus)
- ASTIMAR Golfo & ASTIMAR Pacifico (Naval design, requires respective "Gulf Coast" / "Pacific Coast Naval Yards" focuses)
- Talleres Nacionales de Construcciones Aeronáuticas (Aircraft design, requires "Aviation Workshops" focus)

## Neighbours and diplomatic position

**Borders:** United States (north), Guatemala (south), British Honduras/Belize (southeast). Mexico controls the key Caribbean/Pacific access to Central America.

**Diplomatic:** Guaranteed by the United States under the Monroe Doctrine. Mexico has no faction affiliation in 1936 but can join the Axis, Allies, or Comintern via focus tree.

**Trade:** Exports oil to both the UK and USA, receiving resources in return.

**Expansion Targets:** Central America (Guatemala, Honduras, El Salvador, Nicaragua, Costa Rica), the Caribbean (Cuba, Dominican Republic, Haiti), and the southwestern United States are justified war targets via the focus tree. These objectives align with the historical Mexican Cession and territorial claims.

**Military:** Mexico begins with 21 infantry/cavalry divisions (4 standard Infantry Divisions, 15 Agrarian Militia, 2 Cavalry Divisions), 6 destroyers/coastal defense ships, 45 convoys, and 24 aircraft (6 fighters, 12 CAS, 6 tactical bombers). The navy is minimal; the army is weak but defensible within Mexican territory. Limited industrial capacity requires careful focus on military economy.
