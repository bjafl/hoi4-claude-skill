## Summary

Hearts of Iron IV rewards planning over reflexes. Start by mastering the interface and core loops—production, research, and diplomacy—before engaging in large-scale combat. A beginner should choose a major nation (Germany, Soviet Union, France, or United Kingdom) or an insulated minor to learn at their own pace, focus on economic and technological foundations, and avoid the temptation to rush military buildup.

## Learning Path for New Players

**Inferred:** New players should approach the game in this sequence to build understanding without overwhelm:

### Phase 1: Interface and basic information (first session, Days 1–10 in-game)
- Load a tutorial game or start 1936 as any nation you recognize.
- Learn the top information bar: Political Power (PP), Stability, War Support, Manpower, Factories, Fuel, Logistics Fulfillment, Convoys, Command Power, and Army/Navy/Air Experience.
- Open each menu (Q, W, E, R, T, Y, U, I hotkeys) without making changes. Read what each tab displays.
- Focus on understanding what the numbers mean before optimizing them.

### Phase 2: Production and resources (Days 10–50)
- Open the Production menu (U). You begin with production lines for Infantry Equipment already running.
- Observe that military factories have output (see [Production and Equipment](../01-mechanics/production-and-equipment.md)): **powered military factories produce 4.5 production cost/day at 100% efficiency**, reduced by your current efficiency (typically 10% at the start, growing slowly over time).
- Understand production efficiency: it starts at 10%, has a cap around 50% without research, and grows approximately 1% per 100 days on an unchanged line.
- Build one additional military factory in a high-infrastructure state; then return to watching production.
- Do not attempt to manage all factory output yet; focus on observing how existing lines operate.

### Phase 3: National Focus and political direction (Days 50–150)
- Open the Government menu (Q). A national focus will be highlighted for selection.
- Read the focus tree. Most focuses take 70 days and cost 70 PP (1 PP per day for 70 days).
- For your first game, select a historical focus branch. Germany should pursue Austria/Czechoslovakia claims; France should shore up stability; the Soviet Union should build industry; the United Kingdom should rearm.
- Understand that only one focus can run at a time.

### Phase 4: Research (Days 150–200)
- Open Research (E). You have 2–3 slots to assign technology research.
- Begin with infantry weapon upgrades (one slot) and one industry technology (typically Construction I or Production Efficiency I).
- Avoid researching technologies far ahead of their historical date—there is a penalty (ahead-of-time penalty) that can double or triple research time.

### Phase 5: Diplomacy and war goals (Days 200–365)
- Open Diplomacy (Shift+Q). Study which nations are in which factions (Allies, Axis, Comintern).
- If playing a major power aligned with a faction, join it. If playing a minor, improve relations with a faction leader to enter peacefully.
- Do not declare war without a war goal. A war goal is justified by spending 2+ PP and waiting 100+ days (faster at high world tension).
- Understand that democratic countries require high world tension (80%+) to take aggressive diplomatic actions.

## First Five In-Game Decisions

**Inferred:** Most new players should make these decisions in order:

1. **Pick a major nation or a well-insulated minor.** Germany, Soviet Union, France, United Kingdom, Italy, or Japan are easiest; avoid microstates like Luxembourg or Bhutan. Insulated minors like Mexico, USA (if keeping isolationist), or Australia allow you to prepare without external pressure. Starting with a nation in an active conflict (e.g., China vs Japan, Spain mid-civil-war) adds difficulty; save these for later.

2. **Unlock an extra research slot via national focus.** Most nations have a focus that grants a third research slot within the first 100 days (e.g., Germany's *Expand Education System* or equivalent). Prioritize this over immediate military buildup; research is a force multiplier.

3. **Set conscription law based on ideology, not panic.** 
   - **Fascist or Communist nations** can adopt **Limited Conscription** (2.5% recruitable population, no economic penalties) immediately, then **Extensive Conscription** (5.0% population, still no penalties) once 20% war support is reached.
   - **Democratic nations** start on **Volunteer Only** (1.5% recruitable population) and are locked out of higher levels until 10–20% war support is reached. Do not attempt to rush conscription; focus on efficient production instead.
   - See [Laws and Advisors](../01-mechanics/laws-and-advisors.md) for exact thresholds.

4. **Build 2–3 additional military factories before 1937.** Each military factory produces 4.5 (powered) production cost/day. Early factories accumulate efficiency and become your backbone; delaying factory construction early is costly.
   - Prioritize building in states with high infrastructure (10+) and population (>15) to avoid overcrowding the building cap.
   - Avoid building all factories in one state; spread them across your core territory.

5. **Train divisions to Regular status by exercising in peacetime.** New divisions start at Green experience and suffer 25% penalties to attack, defense, and organization. Exercising at home (select army, click exercise button) costs equipment durability but gives army experience and raises unit status to Regular (~100 days with 5+ divisions exercising). Regular divisions are vastly more effective in early wars.

## Common Early Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

**Inferred:** Most new players repeat these errors; understanding them accelerates learning:

### Mistake 1: Building too many different production lines
**Problem:** Beginners spread military factories across 5+ different equipment types, thinking variety is necessary. This delays reaching efficient production on any one line.

**Why it hurts:** Production efficiency (which applies only to military factories, not dockyards) starts at 10% and grows slowly. Switching a production line to a different equipment type cuts efficiency by 70–90%. Spreading 15 factories across 5 lines (3 factories each) means each line inefficiently reaches 20% efficiency only after months; one consolidated line of 15 factories would reach 40%+ in the same time.

**Inferred fix:** Concentrate military factories. Keep at least 10 factories on Infantry Equipment (your most-used unit type) continuously. Allocate extra factories to other equipment only after infantry has buffered production. See [Production and Equipment](../01-mechanics/production-and-equipment.md) for retention penalties.

### Mistake 2: Ignoring stability and war support
**Problem:** Beginners leave stability below 50% and war support at 0%, not realizing they impose penalties.
- **Stability** below 50% incurs −1% factory output per 1% shortfall. At 30% stability, you lose 20% factory output.
- **War support** affects mobilization speed, command power gain, and conscription law unlocks.

**Why it hurts:** Wasting 20% of your industrial capacity to low stability is equivalent to losing 3 military factories. Worse, you cannot reach higher conscription laws without sufficient war support, crippling manpower in wartime.

**Inferred fix:** In peacetime, ensure stability is at least 50% (neutral). Use national focuses (e.g., "Improve Public Support") to raise it. As war approaches, raises war support with focuses or political advisor traits. See [Laws and Advisors](../01-mechanics/laws-and-advisors.md).

### Mistake 3: Over-reinforcing old divisions instead of building new ones
**Problem:** Beginners reinforce and upgrade existing divisions at 50% equipment allocation, starving new unit recruitment.

**Why it hurts:** A damaged 20-division army at 50% strength is far weaker than one 30-division army at 100%. Equipment sent to reinforcement is not multiplied; a division with 1 rifle in reserve needs 1 rifle to fill that gap. By contrast, a new division with 0 rifles needs all rifles to form.

**Inferred fix:** In wartime, set the "Reinforcement Priority" slider (Recruit & Deploy menu) to the right (toward new units), not left. Let existing divisions stay understrength while you build new ones. After a war ends and units return to peacetime training, then reinforce them. See [Division Designer and Template Rules](../02-land-warfare/division-designer.md).

### Mistake 4: Declaring war too early and without a focus plan
**Problem:** Beginners declare war on neighbors within the first 2 years, before economic or military buildup is complete. Wars drain resources, interrupt focuses, and divide industrial capacity to consumer goods.

**Why it hurts:** A single 70-day national focus that boosts production efficiency or adds research slots is worth more than the early gains of a premature conquest. Once at war, consumer goods factories are allocated by economy law (typically 10–20% of your total), reducing your military output. An early war that lasts 3 years consumes production and manpower that could have built a 50-division army for a later, decisive conflict.

**Inferred fix:** Plan wars as the completion of a focus tree branch. Germany should pursue Austrian and Czechoslovakian annexation via focuses (and their bypass conditions), not early military conquest. Soviet Union should focus on industry and research in the first 3 years, then push into Eastern Europe. Democracies should prepare defensively and declare war only when attacked or allies are threatened.

### Mistake 5: Ignoring division width and combat width
**Problem:** Beginners build divisions with arbitrary width (e.g., 12, 17, or 22 width) and stack unlimited divisions in a province, expecting them all to fight.

**Why it hurts:** Combat occurs in terrain of fixed width (e.g., 70 width in plains, 60 in forest, 50 in mountains). Divisions over that width do not fight; they sit in reserve taking no damage and dealing none. Stacking 10 divisions of unknown width means maybe 3 are fighting, 7 are wasted. See [Land Combat](../02-land-warfare/land-combat.md).

**Inferred fix:** Design divisions of 10 or 20 width (10-width divides neatly into plains/forest/hills at 70/60 width respectively). Build multiple divisions of the same template. In a 70-width plain battle, you can field 7 × 10-width divisions or 3 × 20-width divisions; both are equally useful but give different flexibility. See [Division Designer and Template Rules](../02-land-warfare/division-designer.md).

### Mistake 6: Researching too far ahead of the historical date
**Problem:** Beginners see a tech in the tree and research it immediately, not checking when it was historically developed.

**Why it hurts:** Researching a technology *N* years before its historical date applies an ahead-of-time (AOT) penalty. A 110-day tech researched 5 years early can take 300+ days. An early-game tech rush burns research slots and delays core technologies like Production Efficiency or Infantry upgrades.

**Inferred fix:** Research only techs within 1–2 years of their historical date, or techs with your national focus that grants a large (−50%) speed bonus. Doctrines and industry techs are historically available early and have no or minimal AOT penalty. See [Research and Technology](../01-mechanics/research-and-technology.md).

### Mistake 7: Building a large navy with no strategic purpose
**Problem:** Beginners equate a large navy with military strength and build fleets of capital ships or many submarines.

**Why it hurts:** Naval dockyards are slow (2 production cost/day flat, no efficiency scaling). A capital ship takes 500+ days to produce. By the time your fleet is large, the war may be decided on land. Convoys (used for trade and overseas supply) are cheap and can be built quickly; capital ships are luxury.

**Inferred fix:** In your first games, ignore navy unless you are island-hopping (Japan) or defending a vital convoy route (United Kingdom). Build convoys for trade and overseas supply instead. Once you understand land and air combat, then return to navy. See [Production and Equipment](../01-mechanics/production-and-equipment.md).

### Mistake 8: Forgetting to plan supply lines
**Problem:** Beginners advance deep into enemy territory and watch divisions starve, taking 50% penalties to attack and defense.

**Why it hurts:** Supply is routed backward from your capital through adjacent friendly states. If a long offensive chain is broken (enemy captures a single state behind your lines), all divisions behind that point lose supply. Outnumbered defenders can cut supply lines and stall an entire invasion.

**Inferred fix:** Before attacking, check the supply map (map mode menu, bottom-right). Ensure a continuous chain of friendly states from your capital to the front lines. Hold territory behind you as you advance. Build infrastructure in newly-captured territory (+5 speed, +10% supply flow per level). See [Land Warfare Overview](../02-land-warfare/land-warfare-overview.md).

### Mistake 9: Not using battle plans
**Problem:** Beginners manually issue movement orders to divisions, shift-clicking province by province instead of using the Battle Planner.

**Why it hurts:** Manual movement is slow and divisions arrive tired and disorganized. Battle plans (Shift+click to set defensive line, then Shift+click offensive target) allow divisions to prepare with planning bonus (up to +25% attack after many days) and attack in coordinated waves.

**Inferred fix:** Use the battle planner. Set a defensive front line (front button), then an offensive target (arrow button). Click the green arrow to activate when ready. Divisions will spend days preparing and attack together, increasing odds of breakthrough. See [Land Warfare Overview](../02-land-warfare/land-warfare-overview.md).

### Mistake 10: Failing to adapt to war
**Problem:** Beginners lock in one division template and build thousands of them without adjusting for enemy composition or tactics.

**Why it hurts:** If enemies field heavy tanks, pure infantry divisions cannot pierce their armor and will suffer 50%+ casualty rates. If enemies rely on air superiority, building more infantry does not solve the problem.

**Inferred fix:** Monitor your combat log (Theater menu → document icon) for performance. If infantry is dying to tanks, add some tank destroyers or medium tanks to your templates (requires army XP). If your air is being shot down, add interceptor fighters or increase air wing size. Templates can be edited mid-war without re-training divisions. See [Division Designer and Template Rules](../02-land-warfare/division-designer.md).

## Key Takeaway for New Players

**Inferred:** Treat your first 5 in-game years (1936–1941) as preparation, not conquest. Focus on:
- Unlocking research slots (highest ROI).
- Building factories (production capacity is permanent).
- Training divisions to Regular status (cheap, huge combat boost).
- Researching Production Efficiency and infantry weapons (force multipliers).
- Planning your first war around a focus-tree milestone, not calendar pressure.

Wars are optional until 1939–1941 in most scenarios. Use the lag time to build a war machine. By the time you are forced into conflict, a well-prepared nation will dominate ill-prepared neighbors.
