## Bhutan

Bhutan is a landlocked minor nation in the Eastern Himalayas with minimal starting resources and no initial military. The country begins under the rule of the Wangchuk dynasty (Non-Aligned ideology) but has pathways to all other ideologies. Bhutan uses the generic national focus tree and starts with severe economic and military constraints, making early survival heavily dependent on diplomatic alliances and careful economic management.

It controls extensive tungsten reserves (212 reserves) but limited industrial capacity at game start (1 military factory, 3 civilian factories). As a subject of the United Kingdom, it is locked to democratic ideology by default but can pursue alternative paths through its focus tree or changes in overlord status.

### National spirits

Burma does not have any national spirits at game start.

### Unique mechanics

Burma uses the generic national focus tree (5 branches: Army Effort, Aviation Effort, Naval Effort, Industrial Effort, Political Effort) rather than a country-specific one. The focus tree provides options to shift ideology and unlock additional research slots.

No country-specific decisions or events are documented on the wiki beyond standard political path mechanics.

### Neighbours and diplomatic position

**Borders:**
- West: British Raj (United Kingdom)
- North: Yunnan (independent warlord)
- Northeast: China (Nationalist or other faction)
- East: French Indochina, Siam
- South: Sea access

**Status:**
- Colony (subject) of the United Kingdom at game start
- Can gain independence if Britain is forced to decolonize (e.g., through military defeat or specific events)
- Vulnerable to Japanese invasion once Japan enters the war (similar to other British Southeast Asian territories); wiki notes Burma can be prone to invasion by Yunnan if independent
- 1 air base and 2 naval bases provide minimal defensive infrastructure

Burma's strategic value lies in its tungsten reserves and its position blocking Japanese access to India via Siam. Historically, it became a major battleground in WWII after Japan's 1942 invasion, leading to occupation and a Japanese-backed puppet government (State of Burma under Ba Maw). The wiki notes that Ba Maw's fascist path mirrors this historical trajectory, while U Nu's democratic path reflects post-war independence movements.

Its strategic location and Singapore's natural harbor make it vital for Asian-Pacific dominance, though as a puppet it has limited independence unless it breaks free through civil war or political action.

### Government and political situation

**Other political parties:**
- **Malayan Communist Party** (33% popularity, Marxism) — leader Lai Teck (trait: Smooth-Talking Charmer, −50% improve relations cost, +10% trade opinion)

- **Panoceanic Unionists** (0% popularity, Fascism) — leader Winston Tyron (trait: Tenacious Negotiator, +10% political power gain, +25% trade opinion)

**Puppet status:** As an Integrated Puppet, British Malaya cannot normally declare war and is subordinate to UK decisions. Breaking free requires either completing "Increase Autonomy" Continuous Focus (very slow) or triggering a civil war through ideology shifts and stability reduction.

### Focus tree and mechanics

British Malaya lacks a unique national focus tree and uses the **generic focus tree** instead, with five main branches:
- **Army Effort** — army experience and army research bonuses
- **Aviation Effort** — air experience, air bases, air research bonuses
- **Naval Effort** — navy experience, naval dockyards, navy research bonuses
- **Industrial Effort** — civilian and military factories, infrastructure, +2 research slots
- **Political Effort** — choices for ideology path

No unique mechanics documented. Strategic play typically focuses on civil war tactics to break UK control: accumulate military factories and army experience via focus tree, then trigger ideology shift to Fascism or Communism, incite civil war, and fight for control of Singapore before independence.

### Neighbours and diplomatic position

**Borders:** Siam (north), Dutch East Indies (south).

**Faction:** British bloc (as UK puppet). Guarantees from the UK protect British Malaya from aggression, but prevent independent military action.

**Strategic notes:** Singapore (in Malaya state) is described as vital for dominance in Asia-Pacific. Control of Singapore becomes increasingly contested once Japan begins expanding. The island is a natural choke point for trade between the Indian and Pacific Oceans.

## Nepal

Nepal is a landlocked minor country in the Himalayas, bordered by the British Raj to the south, east, and west, and Tibet to the north. With minimal industrial capacity and only one state, Nepal has very limited expansion opportunities and must pursue a careful diplomatic strategy to survive major-power competition in South Asia.

**Resources (1936):** Chromium 3. All other resources unavailable.

### Government and political situation

Nepal starts as a Non-Aligned authoritarian monarchy under **Juddha Rana** of the Royal House of Gorkha, with no elections. The country maintains a neutral foreign policy historically.

Four political parties exist with alternative ideologies:
- **Democratic:** Bhakti Thapa — would rename the country to Federal Republic of Nepal
- **Communist:** Bir Shamsher Singh Thapa — would rename to Federal Democratic Republic of Nepal
- **Fascist:** Amar Thapa — would rename to Gorkha Empire
- **Non-Aligned (ruling):** Juddha Rana — maintains Nepal as the Kingdom

The wiki does not document specific political conversion paths available through the focus tree or internal decisions.

### National spirits

**Royal Nepalese Army**

### Unique mechanics

Nepal lacks a unique national focus tree and uses the generic focus tree instead, which offers five main branches: Army Effort, Aviation Effort, Naval Effort, Industrial Effort, and Political Effort. No special decisions or country-specific mechanics are documented on the wiki.

### Geography and military

Nepal owns a single mountainous state with Kathmandu as its capital. The terrain is entirely mountain (7 provinces). The country starts with **2 mountain divisions** (Paidala Dibhijana) and 16,000 manpower. It has no navy or air force.

Adjacent to mountainous terrain, Nepal's military is naturally suited to mountain warfare, reflected in the Royal Nepalese Army spirit's special forces bonuses.

### Diplomacy and borders

Nepal borders:

- **Tibet** (north)
- **Bhutan** (east, separated by the Indian state of West Bengal)

The country pursues a neutral foreign policy and is not part of any faction at game start.

## Gansu Ma

Gansu Ma is a northwestern Chinese warlord state ruled by Ma Buqing and part of the Ma Clique faction. One of the weakest nations in the game with no factories or resources, it must immediately develop its economy and pursue annexation of neighbouring Ma warlords to achieve viability. As a minor power it can align with the Chinese Nationalists, Communists, or attempt independence through the Opposition branch.

| Jiuquan | Jiuquan | Desert (4), Mountain (2) | 550,000 | 1 |

### Government and political situation

Gansu Ma is ruled by the Ma Clique under Ma Buqing with despotic government. The national spirit Warlord State reflects the limitations of warlord governance, imposing penalties to political power gain, stability, and research speed while improving division attrition and recruitable population.

Alternative ideologies are available through focus completion: China Democratic League offers Socialism, the Communist Party of China permits Anti-Revisionism, and the Blue Shirt Society enables Rexism. All ideologies available through the Opposition branch focus "Secure Internal Politics". Ma Buqing holds the "Staunch Anti-Communist" trait regardless of ideology, reducing communist diplomacy acceptance and support.

### National spirits

Gansu Ma starts with four national spirits typical of warlord states:

| Spirit | Effects |
|--------|---------|
| **Commonplace Looting** | Consumer Goods Factories factor: +10.0% / Division Recovery Rate: +5.0% / Construction Speed: -5.00% |
| **Government Corruption** | Political Power Gain: -10% / Stability: -10.00% |
| **Ineffective Bureaucracy** | Daily Political Power Gain: -0.25 |
| **Warlord State** | Political Power Gain: -5% / Recruitable Population Factor: +5% / Division Attrition: -5.0% / Supply Consumption: -3.0% / Stability: -5.00% / Research Speed: -3.00% / Daily Command Power Gain Multiplier: -15% |

The **Ma Clique Collective** spirit (shared with other Ma warlords) grants +5.0% Cavalry Attack.

The Warlord State spirit is removed upon completion of the focuses "Power Struggle", "The Yan'an Incident", or "Proclaim Rival Government".

### Unique mechanics

Gansu Ma has access to both shared and exclusive focus branches:

- **Secure Internal Politics:** Opening branch covering all political paths (Nationalist cooperation, Communist cooperation, or Opposition/independence).
- **Develop the Capital State:** Industrial development branch.
- **Strengthen Warlord Authority:** Modifies Warlord State spirit and grants cavalry or mountaineer bonuses.
- **Sideline Family Conflicts:** Exclusive Ma Clique branch permitting demands/war goals against Khotan Ma and Qinghai Ma, enabling annexation and coring toward forming Xibei San Ma. Gansu Ma and Ningxia Ma lack individual exclusive branches within this system.
- **Develop Inter-Province Ties:** Shared Ma Clique branch that ultimately permits formation of the faction "Ma Clique Collective" with other Ma warlords.

At the end of the focus tree, Gansu Ma can pivot to either the Nationalist Chinese Warlord or Communist Chinese Warlord focus branches depending on ideology and focus choices.

Design companies available include Sino-German Technical Mission (heavy tank designer, requires peace with Germany or German Military Advisors spirit), Morozov Design Bureau (mobile tank designer, requires Soviet Designer focus and peace with USSR), Jiangnan Shipyard Group and Dagu Shipyard (naval designers), CAMCO divisions (aircraft designers), and various arsenal designers (Taiyuan, Liaoning, Hanyan, Mukden).

See [06-focus-trees/china-warlords.md](../06-focus-trees/china-warlords.md) for the shared warlord focus tree.

## Hebei-Chahar

With a large population but minimal industry, it begins under Kuomintang rule and faces pressure from the Japanese Empire, Manchukuo, and Mengjiang to the north. Despite severe starting disadvantages, it has a unique national focus tree enabling transformation into the Khotan Emirate and pursuit of an Islamic sultanate path separate from the main Chinese political factions.

The two home states—Guangxi and Nanning—are situated in developed rural regions with mixed terrain (hills, mountains, plains, forest, urban). Without the No Compromise, No Surrender DLC, Guangxi also owns Guangdong's states, multiplying available industry and resources.

### Government and political situation

**Starting government**: New Guangxi Clique (led by Li Zongren), a unique warlord faction.

The Guangxi Clique begins under Li Zongren, one of China's most powerful warlords and a historical rival to Chiang Kai-Shek. Li leads a politically fractured state with minimal stability and significant bureaucratic corruption. The ruling party can transition to other ideologies via the national focus tree:

- **Nationalism path**: Complete "Cooperation With the Nationalists" branch to integrate with Nationalist China and inherit its focus tree.
- **Communism path**: Complete "Cooperation With the Communists" branch to align with the Chinese Soviet Republic and eventually replace it with Soviet support.
- **Rival government path**: Complete "Opposition" branch to establish an independent warlord government aligned with either Nationalist or Communist ideology based on party popularity.

Li Zongren's traits as leader—Fierce Leader (provides +15% Surrender Limit, +10% Division Defense on core territory)—make him effective for defensive play but not essential for early expansion.

### National spirits

**All warlords (base game) start with:**

1. **Government Corruption**: −10% Political Power Gain, −10% Stability
2. **Ineffective Bureaucracy**: −0.25 daily Political Power
3. **Warlord State**: −5% Political Power Gain, +5% Recruitable Population, −5% Division Attrition, −3% Supply Consumption, −5% Stability, −3% Research Speed, −15% Daily Command Power Gain. Removed upon completing "Power Struggle," "The Yan'an Incident," or "Proclaim Rival Government" focus.

**With No Compromise, No Surrender DLC, additionally:**

4. **Local Unease**: −5% Political Power, −10% Stability
5. **Commonplace Looting**: +10% Consumer Goods Factories, +5% Division Recovery Rate, −5% Construction Speed

These spirits impose heavy penalties on political power generation and command power, balancing warlord states' access to large manpower and border conflicts. The "Warlord State" spirit removal is a critical decision point in the focus tree.

### Unique mechanics

**Warlord state designation**: Guangxi is part of the Chinese Warlords national focus tree (shared with other warlord cliques: Yunnan, Shandong, Sichuan, Guangdong, Shanxi, Hebei-Chahar, Ningxia Ma, Gansu Ma, Qinghai Ma, Khotan Ma, Sinkiang). The tree is divided into branches for political alignment (Nationalist, Communist, opposition) and industrial development ("Develop the Capital State").

**Border Conflicts**: As a warlord, Guangxi can initiate border conflicts with neighboring warlords and Nationalist China at low world tension, seizing states without formal war. This is the primary expansionist mechanism for warlords early game.

**Japanese Visit branch** (No Compromise, No Surrender only): Guangxi shares a unique joint focus branch with Guangdong Clique that prepares the Liangguang Incident, a confrontation with Chiang Kai-Shek's Nationalist government.

**Conditional DLC content**: Several design companies (e.g., Nanjing Wireless Electronics Plant, Shanghai Electronics/Power Company, Bank of China) require No Compromise, No Surrender or specific focus completions and may not be available to players without the DLC. Companies accessing states outside Guangxi's core (Shanghai, Liaoning, Shanxi) require territorial control via conquest or political unification.

**No unique mechanics documented for Guangxi beyond shared warlord mechanics** beyond the joint branch with Guangdong Clique.

### Neighbours and diplomatic position

**Bordering countries**:
- **Guangdong Clique** (east/south): Only borders Guangxi without No Compromise, No Surrender DLC. With the DLC, is integrated into Guangxi's territory at start.
- **Yunnan** (west): Warlord state, can ally through United Front or conquered via border conflicts.
- **China** (north): Nationalist China, the primary rival. Border conflicts with China are possible and strategic.
- **French Indochina** (southwest): Colonial subject of France; no initial border conflicts possible.

**Military status** (1939 onward): Guangxi, like all Chinese warlords and Nationalist China, is at war with Japan and its puppets (Manchukuo, Mengjiang).

**Starting alignment**: No faction. Guangxi can join or support the United Front (via Nationalist unification) or align with the Comintern (via Communist conversion). Under the Opposition focus branch, Guangxi can establish a separate rival warlord government and remain outside both major Communist and Nationalist frameworks until late game.

**Diplomatic position**: At start, Guangxi has no allies or guarantees. Potential for non-aggression pacts or trade deals with neighbors. In multiplayer or later singleplayer, Guangxi often becomes a focal point for warlord diplomatic plays—either conquered by Nationalist/Communist China or unified with other warlords to form a super-warlord state. The Liangguang Incident (with DLC) provides a joint challenge with Guangdong against the Nationalists.
