The Kingdom of Hungary is a landlocked minor power in Eastern Europe, ruled by regent Miklós Horthy under the Non-Aligned ideology. It begins the game economically and militarily constrained by the Treaty of Trianon, but possesses territorial claims on neighbouring Czechoslovakia and Romania, and maintains strong political currents toward fascism that can be activated through its national focus tree.

**States:** Bács-Kiskun, North Transdanubia, Northern Hungary (contains Budapest), South Transdanubia, Transtisza.

**Ideology:** Non-Aligned (44% support for ruling Nemzeti Egység Pártja; 31% fascist support; 25% socialist; 0% communist).

**Starting Government:** Miklós Horthy as regent. Elections scheduled for March 1939. Hungary is neither democratic nor communist at game start, but has strong latent fascist sympathies and can be swung toward either extreme or democracy via the focus tree.

## Government and political situation

Miklós Horthy, the regent, heads the government with strong anti-communist views and acceptance of fascist diplomacy. Horthy's position is precarious: the Treaty of Trianon limits Hungary's military and naval expansion, creating domestic pressure for revision. The country is economically dependent on German trade (the Gömbös Trade Treaty provides arms deals with Germany but incurs penalties to political power gain and consumer goods).

The focus tree offers multiple paths:
- **End the Regency**: Elect either a democratic king (Carl V Wilhelm of Sweden), a fascist king (Friedrich Franz of Mecklenburg-Schwerin), or a Habsburg non-aligned monarch (Otto von Habsburg). This path opens the Austro-Hungarian union route.
- **Continue the Regency**: Reinforce Horthy's rule or undermine him (fascist path). The historical path leads to alignment with Germany and creation of a Greater Hungary.
- **End the White Terror**: Transition to a democratic or communist republic, potentially joining the Comintern.

Notable alternative leaders include Otto von Habsburg (Habsburg restoration), István Horthy (Horthy's son, air-focused), Ferenc Szálasi (radical fascist), and Mátyás Rákosi (communist).

## Unique mechanics

**Territorial Claims and Revision:** Hungary begins with claims on Southern Slovakia and Carpathian Ruthenia (Czechoslovakia). The focus tree allows claims on Transylvania (Romania), Vojvodina and West Banat (Yugoslavia), and other lands. The Little Entente (Czechoslovakia, Romania, Yugoslavia) can be negotiated with; arbitration by a third power determines territorial transfers.

**Vienna Awards:** The historical path references the First Vienna Award (November 1938, gains Southern Slovakia) and Second Vienna Award (September 1940, gains Northern Transylvania). These are resolved through focus tree mechanics and depend on great-power arbitration.

**Greater Hungary:** The fascist path allows Hungary to renounce the Treaty of Trianon and demand Bled Agreement concessions, then expand to recreate a larger Hungarian state. This requires military strength and German support, and can escalate into a multi-front war against Romania and Yugoslavia.

**Austro-Hungarian Union:** The Habsburg path (Otto or Joseph August von Habsburg) opens joint focus branches with Austria, allowing Hungary and Austria to rebuild the Austro-Hungarian Empire, gain claims across the Balkans and Western Europe, and form a superpower with shared bonuses to military and industry. Czechoslovakia and Romania can be reintegrated as associated states.

**Focus Tree Branches** (Götterdämmerung version):
- **End the Regency / Continue the Regency:** Split routes for monarchy vs. regency.
- **End the White Terror:** Democratic or communist paths.
- **Avoid Interventionist Destabilization / Economic Intervention:** Industrial and economic branches.
- **Secret Rearmament:** Military research and technology branches.
- **Indivisible and Inseparable (Joint Austro-Hungarian):** Multi-nation cooperative branch with Austria, unlocked after specific focuses.

## Neighbours and diplomatic position

Hungary borders Austria (west), Czechoslovakia (north), Romania (east), and Yugoslavia (south). It has no direct access to the sea.

**Alliances & Claims:** Hungary begins with claims on Czechoslovakia and can generate claims on Romania (Transylvania) and Yugoslavia (Vojvodina, West Banat). Germany becomes Hungary's primary ally if the country pursues fascism; the Gömbös Trade Treaty locks Hungary into German economic dependence early. The Allies and Soviet Union are distant diplomatic partners until Hungary's ideological path determines allegiance.

**Little Entente Pressure:** Czechoslovakia, Romania, and Yugoslavia form a defensive alliance against Hungarian and Italian expansion. Hungary can break this alliance by growing stronger militarily or through diplomatic manipulation (improving relations, demanding territory through arbitration rather than war).
