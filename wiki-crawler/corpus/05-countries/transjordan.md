A British colony in the Middle East with minimal industry and population. Transjordan is a dependency of the United Kingdom as of 1936, but can achieve independence through British colonial policy reforms or political transitions. The nation has one state, no military, and zero resources, making it unsuitable for conquest but viable as a puppet or minor Allied contribution.

## Government and political situation

Transjordan begins as a Non-Aligned Emirate under the Hashemite dynasty, ruled by Emir **Abdullah bin al-Hussein** (trait: Emir of Transjordan; grants +10% Stability and +5% Political Power Gain). The ruling Hashemite party controls 50% popularity.

Three opposition parties offer alternative paths:
- **National Liberal Party** (Democratic, 25% popularity, led by Hashim Kheir)
- **Jordanian Phalange** (Fascist, 20% popularity, led by Saeed Ahmadi)
- **Jordanian Workers Society** (Communist, 5% popularity, led by Fu'ad Nassar)

Elections occur every 4 years; the next is January 1940. Transjordan begins as a British colony and can become independent through Britain's "Revisit Colonial Policy" decision or by changing ideology to Communism or Fascism to join the Comintern or Axis.

## National spirits

Transjordan has no unique national spirits.

## Unique mechanics

**Generic national focus tree only** — Transjordan uses the generic focus tree with five branches: Army Effort, Aviation Effort, Naval Effort, Industrial Effort, and Political Effort.

**Arabia formation** — As an Arab country, Transjordan can attempt to form Arabia by conquering the necessary territories.

**British colony status** — Begins as a British possession. Can be released as independent by Britain via colonial policy or claimed/released by other powers in peace deals. Independent play unlocks more strategic options but severely limits resources and industrial capacity.

### Strategic notes
Transjordan has a coastline on the Red Sea but no naval bases, requiring construction before deploying naval units or conducting naval invasions. The nation contributes minimal manpower to any war effort due to limited population (much of it nomadic) and complete lack of strategic resources. Lend-lease arrangements with Britain or membership in a foreign faction are necessary to conduct any meaningful military activity. The single state and two building slots severely restrict industrial expansion without external assistance.

Historically, Transjordan declared war on the Axis in 1939 alongside Britain and later contributed the Arab Legion to the British invasion of Iraq in 1941.
