Cuba is a small island nation in the Caribbean with minimal industrial capacity and limited resources, making it one of the weakest starting positions in the game. Its economy relies heavily on agriculture and trade, while its military is symbolic rather than substantial. Protected by the Monroe Doctrine guarantee from the United States, Cuba must navigate between ideological pressures and regional dynamics.

## Government and political situation

Cuba starts as a **Democratic** nation (formally: republic) under the **National Union** party, led by **José Agripino Barnet**. Elections are scheduled for January 1940. The country's political landscape is fragmented but stable:

| Party | Ideology | Popularity | Leader | Country Name |
|-------|----------|-----------|--------|--------------|
| National Union | Democratic | 98% | José Agripino Barnet | Cuba |
| Partido Comunista Cubano (PCC) | Communist | 2% | Guillermo Cabral | Revolutionary Cuba |
| Fascist Party | Fascist | 0% | Wenceslao Carmona | Falangist Cuba |
| Non-Aligned | Non-Aligned | 0% | Gregorio Morales | Republic of Cuba |

**Alternative Leaders** (accessible through ideology shifts):
- **Socialist** (1939): Federico Laredo Brú (National Union)
- **Communist**: Guillermo Cabral (Partido Comunista Cubano) — carries trait *Reluctant Interventionist* (+5% Stability, -20% lend-lease tension limit)
- **Fascist**: Wenceslao Carmona
- **Non-Aligned**: Gregorio Morales (traits: *Economist* — +5% production efficiency cap, -5% consumer goods factories)

Cuba's single state prevents civil war, so ideology transitions must be peaceful. The generic focus tree's Political Effort branch provides the path to other ideologies.

## National spirits

**Monroe Doctrine** (starting spirit): The independence of Cuba is guaranteed by the United States. This spirit provides protection but limits strategic autonomy and ties Cuba's interests to US interventionism.

## Economic laws and trade

**Starting Laws (1936)**
- Conscription: Volunteer Only (1.5% recruitable population)
- Trade: Export Focus (+10% construction speed, +5% research speed, +10% factory/dockyard output, 50% resources to market)
- Economy: Civilian Economy (35% expected factories on consumer goods, +30% factory conversion cost, -30% factory energy, -40% fuel per oil, -25% fuel capacity, -30% factory construction speed)

The Export Focus trade law means 50% of resources flow to external trade, significantly limiting available domestic supplies.

## Neighbours and diplomatic position

**Geography**: Cuba is located south of the United States, west of Hispaniola (Haiti/Dominican Republic). It comprises a single island state with varied terrain: 7 plains provinces, 2 hills, 1 mountain. Major cities are Guantánamo and Havana (Havana capital, 15 victory points).

**Diplomatic Status**: The Monroe Doctrine guarantee from the United States provides defensive protection but also limits strategic options. As a minor nation with minimal military power, Cuba's diplomatic role centers on trade relationships and avoiding great-power conflicts in the Western Hemisphere.
