Turkey is a regional power in the Eastern Mediterranean and Middle East, controlling the strategically vital Bosporus. In 1936, it is governed by the nationalist Cumhuriyet Halk Partisi (CHP) under Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, pursuing a policy of neutrality and internal consolidation. The country struggles with ideological divisions between Kemalist secularists, religious traditionalists, and Kurdish populations seeking autonomy.

## Government and political situation

**Starting government:** Non-Aligned under Mustafa Kemal Atatürk (Cumhuriyet Halk Partisi), with 94% party popularity. Atatürk holds the trait "Father of the Turks," providing +30% stability and significant reductions to advisor and conscription law costs.

**Other ideologies available:**
- **Democratic (6%):** Müstakil Grup (Independent Group) led by Celâl Bayar
- **Fascist (0%):** Faşist Konsey led by Mehmet Recep Peker
- **Communist (0%):** Türkiye Komünist Partisi led by Şefik Hüsnü

**Political succession:** Atatürk's death (event) or retirement (decision) triggers succession crises. Possible heirs include İsmet İnönü (Oligarchic), Fevzi Çakmak (Despotism), and Kazım Orbay (Despotism), each with distinct bonuses. Alternative paths lead to Ottoman restoration (Despotism) or election outcomes. Celâl Bayar and Adnan Menderes can come to power via Democratic or Centrist routes.

## National spirits

**Dynamic spirits** (changeable via decisions and focuses):

**Kemalist Officers** — The military faction, with Power (1–8+) and Loyalty (1–3) variables. Base form (Limited Power, Loyal) grants:
- Army experience gain: -10%

## Unique mechanics

**State Modifiers:** Internal divisions are reinforced by state-level modifiers. Kemalists dominate western and northern states; Traditionalists in central and southern regions; Kurds in the southeast (Diyarbakır, Hakkari, Van, Tunceli). These interact with decisions and focuses to shift support or trigger civil war if tensions spike.

**Focus tree (requires Battle for the Bosporus DLC):** Without the DLC, Turkey uses the generic focus tree. With it, the unique tree includes:
- **Saadabad Pact Branch** (joint with Afghanistan, Iraq, Iran): builds up "The Saadabad Pact" spirit with industry and military bonuses
- **Montreux Convention Branch** (6 sub-sections): internal politics; sub-branches to integrate Kurds, privatize state enterprises, adopt Fascism/Communism, or pursue Ottoman restoration
- **Foreign policy branches** (Allies, Axis, Comintern): alliance bonuses and war goals against regional rivals
- **Misak-ı Millî (National Pact):** territorial expansion into Soviet, Middle Eastern, Eastern European, and Chinese territories; creates the Turanian Empire

See [06-focus-trees/turkey.md](../06-focus-trees/turkey.md) for the full focus tree.

**Events:** Turkish events cover internal crises (Ottoman restoration, Kurdish integration), succession upon Atatürk's death, and external diplomacy. [Events detailed on wiki but not exhaustively covered here.]

**Decisions:** Citizens and State tabs include decisions to entrench Kemalism, suppress Kurdish factions, retire Atatürk, alter royal laws, and manage industrial monopolies (e.g., Keçi Ticaret A.Ş., Sümerbank, Etibank). Many trigger focuses or alter spirits.

## Neighbours and diplomatic position

**Borders:** Bulgaria and Greece (west), Italy via Rhodes (southwest), Soviet Union (northeast), Syria (southeast), Iraq (south), Iran (east), UK via Cyprus (Mediterranean south). Black Sea to north, Mediterranean and Aegean to south and west.

**Starting faction:** Unaligned (can join Comintern, Axis, or Allies via focus).

**Notable diplomatic features:**
- Historically signed a Mutual Aid Pact with France and UK (1939) but remained neutral after German invasion of France (fearing Soviet retaliation).
- Chromite is a critical trade resource; Allies and Axis both seek to control its sale.
- Non-aggression pact with Germany (June 1941) after Bulgaria joined Axis.
- Entered the war on the Allies' side in February 1945 (for UN membership, not combat).

No guarantees mentioned; diplomatic position is highly malleable via the focus tree.
