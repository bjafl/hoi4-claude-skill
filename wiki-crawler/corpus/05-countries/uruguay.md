Uruguay is a minor South American nation under authoritarian rule in 1936. A small, geographically isolated state bordered only by Brazil and Argentina, it begins with minimal military capacity but benefits from U.S. guarantees under the Monroe Doctrine. Political instability creates paths to democratic or fascist ideologies, and its strategic position makes it a useful base for South American campaigns.

## Government and political situation

Uruguay begins 1936 under Gabriel Terra's dictatorship (Non-Aligned ideology). Terra and the Partido Nacional control 53% political support, while the opposition Partido Colorado (Democratic, 44%) remains sidelined. A small Communist party (3%) and an embryonic Fascist movement (0%) complete the political landscape.

### Party leaders and transitions

**Starting regime (Terra's Dictatorship, Non-Aligned):**
- Gabriel Terra (Partido Nacional, Despotic trait) — +0.01 daily support for Unaligned and Democracy

**Possible transitions from elections (April 1938):**
- Luis Alberto de Herrera (Partido Nacional, Moderatism) — pan-Americanist caudillo; +10% max command power, +10% division defense on core territory, -50% justify war goal time
- Alfredo Baldomir Ferrari (Partido Colorado, Liberalism) — pro-Allied politician; +0.03 daily Democracy support, +15 acceptance of Democratic diplomacy, +10% political power gain, +5% war support

**Alternative path (requires political subversion):**
- Eugenio Gómez (Partido Comunista, Stalinism) — via Military Coup Attempt event; +10% factory output, +15% ideology drift defense
- Arnulf Fuhrmann (Partido Nacista, Fascism) — via The Fuhrmann Plan focus; -30% improve relations cost, +0.05 daily Fascism support, +10% political power gain, +5% war support

Elections recur every 4 years. The 1938 election is a key decision point: Blancos lead toward agricultural/rural industrialization with flexible war declarations; Colorados pursue foreign investment while maintaining Allied alignment.

## Unique mechanics

Uruguay gains a unique national focus tree as part of the Trial of Allegiance DLC. Without the DLC, it uses the generic focus tree.

### Focus tree branches (Trial of Allegiance)

**Terra's Dictatorship Branch:**
Industrial capacity expansion under Gabriel Terra's consolidated rule. Includes a sub-branch to break relations with the Soviet Union and option to support Fascism via Axis backing and volunteers to Spanish Civil War.

**Post-1938 Election Branches (Blancos vs. Colorados):**
- **Blancos Election Victory:** Rural, manual industrial development; allows declaring war on same-ideology countries, justifying on non-WT-generating opponents, and joining factions with other ideologies.
- **Colorado Election Victory:** Foreign investment focus; remains neutral and joins the Allies.

**Shared Branches:**
Paraguay/Uruguay shared military and expansion focuses grant building bonuses and research speed increases. Trial of Allegiance adds special national spirits and military bonuses tied to allied choice (shared military branches).

See [06-focus-trees/uruguay.md](../06-focus-trees/uruguay.md) for the full focus tree.

## Neighbours and diplomatic position

### Bordering countries
- Brazil (north)
- Argentina (west)

### Diplomatic guarantees
- **Guaranteed by:** United States (Monroe Doctrine)
- **Trade law:** Export Focus (base game default: +10% construction, +5% research, +10% factory/dockyard output; 50% resources to market)
- **Economy law:** Civilian Economy (base game default: 35% factories on consumer goods, −30% factory conversion cost, −40% energy consumption)

### Strategic position
Isolated from major powers by geography and ocean. Monroe Doctrine protection means the U.S. will not attack, but also prevents joining the Axis or Comintern without triggering U.S. hostility. Serves as a staging point for Caribbean/South American naval operations and potential base for volunteers to Spain during the Civil War.
