## Government and political situation

Guatemala starts as a Non-Aligned state under the authoritarian rule of General Jorge Ubico, who maintains a 70% popularity base. The primary political opposition is the Progressive Liberal Party (Democratic, 30%), with Communist and Fascist parties at 0% popularity but available for conversion. No elections occur in the non-aligned path.

**Available leaders:**
- **Justo Arbenz** (Democratic, Progressive Liberal Party) — Conservatism ideology
- **Máximo Arbenz** (Communist) — Anti-Revisionism ideology; traits: Deal Broker, Smooth-Talking Charmer
- **Diego Barahona** (Fascist) — Rexism ideology; trait: National Integralist
- **Jorge Ubico** (Non-Aligned, starting) — Despotism ideology

## National spirits

**Monroe Doctrine**  
Guatemala's independence is guaranteed by the United States. This spirit represents the geopolitical protection afforded by the Monroe Doctrine, ensuring the US will defend against external aggression toward Central American nations.

## Unique mechanics

Guatemala has no unique national focus tree and uses the generic tree instead, offering five branches: Army Effort (army research), Aviation Effort (air bases, air research), Naval Effort (naval dockyards, navy research), Industrial Effort (factories, infrastructure, research slots), and Political Effort (ideology conversion).

Guatemala may form the **United States of Central America** by controlling all independent Central American nations (excluding Panama). Doing so grants cores on El Salvador, Honduras, Costa Rica, Nicaragua, and British Mexico (state 311).

## Neighbours and diplomatic position

Guatemala borders Mexico to the north; El Salvador and Honduras to the south; and the United Kingdom (via British Honduras) to the east. The Monroe Doctrine guarantee from the United States provides nominal protection against external aggression. The nation is ideally positioned to consolidate Central America by annexing its five neighbors and forming the regional union.
