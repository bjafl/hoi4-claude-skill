The Japanese Empire was the military-driven imperial power of East Asia, commanding extensive territories across mainland Japan, Korea, Manchuria, Taiwan, and the Pacific. Its government oscillated between nominal civilian rule and militarism under the shadow of the Emperor, with deep factional divides between the Army and Navy determining much of its strategy and doctrine. Japan begins in 1936 as a Non-Aligned Militarist state but has historical focus tree branches leading into Fascism under the banner of the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere.

Major core states include Kanto (Tokyo), Kansai (Osaka), Tokai (Nagoya), and Hokkaido. Colonial possessions span Korea, Taiwan, and strategic Pacific islands including Iwo Jima, Saipan, and Guam. Japan controls puppet states Manchukuo and (by 1939) Mengjiang across northern China.

## Government and political situation

Japan begins under **Keisuke Okada** (Kyokoku Itchi Naikasu, Militarism ideology), a moderate politician whose stability is offset by weak war support. His traits grant +10% Political Power gain but -5% Stability and -5% War support.

Four competing ideologies contend for influence:
- **Non-Aligned (40%):** Kyokoku Itchi Naikasu — ruling party
- **Fascist (35%):** Kokumin Dōmei, led by Kenzō Adachi  
- **Democratic (23%):** Rikken Minseito, led by Tetsu Katayama
- **Communist (2%):** Nihon Kyōsan-tō (Kōza-ha), led by Kyuichi Tokuda

Alternative starting leaders are available for other ideologies (Adachi for Fascism, Tokuda for Communism). The focus tree enables civil wars and ideology flips; Democratic and Communist paths trigger civil conflicts that cede Manchukuo's independence to the puppet state. The Fascist "Purge the Kodoha Faction" branch leads to Prime Ministers Hideki Tōjō or Isoroku Yamamoto, consolidating military control.

### Imperial Influence (No Compromise No Surrender)

The No Compromise, No Surrender DLC introduces the **Imperial Influence** mechanic simulating Japan's four internal factions—Army, Navy, Zaibatsu, and Civic—competing for dominance under the Emperor. Factions gain or lose influence through events, decisions, and focuses, dynamically modifying national modifiers:

- **Meddling Army Faction:** Infantry/Support/CAS/Artillery production cost: +2.5% | Army Fuel Capacity: −2.5%
- **Meddling Navy Faction:** Carrier Aircraft/Warship production cost: +2.5% | Navy Fuel Consumption: +2.5%
- **Meddling Zaibatsu Faction:** Economy Laws Cost: +20% | Infrastructure construction speed: +10% | Civilian factory construction speed: +10%
- **Meddling Civic Faction:** Daily Political Power Gain: +0.05 | Recruitable population: −5% | Stability: +5% | War support: −10% | Trade Laws Cost: +25%

## Unique mechanics

**Imperial Army and Navy Doctrine:** Japan's military is historically split between two power centers with distinct equipment and research preferences. The Imperial Army spirit grants jungle warfare bonuses (reflecting Pacific campaigns) but penalizes armor doctrine initially; the Imperial Navy grants range and torpedo bonuses. The focus tree allows players to gradually consolidate or exacerbate these divisions.

**Kamikaze Strikes (Non-Aligned Monarchist path):** The "Support the Kodoha Faction" branch unlocks Suicide Bomb Craft—unique aircraft that deal extremely high damage but consume both pilot and aircraft. Requires the Non-Aligned Monarchist branch and specific focus completion.

**Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere:** The Fascist historical path allows Japan to build a trade bloc and integrate puppet states (Manchukuo, Mengjiang, others) through decisions and focuses. Completion opens opportunities to annex and core new Pacific territories.

**Hokushin-ron (Northern Expansion) and Nanshin-ron (Southern Expansion) focus sub-branches:** Grant bonuses for expansion into Siberia (northward) or Southeast Asia/Pacific (southward), with associated temporary peace treaties and resource gains upon conquest.

**Zaibatsu mechanic:** Large Japanese industrial groups (Mitsui, Mitsubishi, Sumitomo, Yasuda) can be leveraged through the Ministry of Commerce and Industry focuses to reduce focus duration and provide economic bonuses, with the Zaibatsu Faction gaining influence accordingly.

**Cosmetic equipment names:** Japan has unique names for all infantry equipment, tanks, naval ships, and aircraft spanning the war years (Type 38 rifle, Type 99 LMG, Ha-Go light tank, Ki-27 fighter, etc.), reflecting historical Japanese designations. See the wiki page for full tables.

## Neighbours and diplomatic position

**Bordering nations:**
- Soviet Union (shared Sakhalin border, contested northern Manchuria frontier)
- China (via puppet Manchukuo; direct border after Chinese invasion campaigns)
- Puppets: Manchukuo, Mengjiang (from 1939)

**Starting faction:** Non-aligned; unaligned at game start but historically gravitates toward Axis (Tripartite Pact proposed in the Fascist branch).

**Guarantees:** None at start, but the London Naval Treaty constrains naval production and tech until renounced.

**Alliances:** The "Purge the Kodoha Faction" focus leads to proposing the Tripartite Pact with fascist European powers (Germany, Italy); Democratic and Communist branches offer paths to join the Allies or form alternative factions (West Pacific Treaty Organisation for democrats).

Japan's strategic position rests on naval supremacy in the Pacific and resource conquest in Asia. Early aggression against China and later against the Soviet Union (Hokushin-ron), Southeast Asia (Nanshin-ron), or both are the game's central military options.
